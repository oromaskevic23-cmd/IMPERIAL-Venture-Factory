# Customer Evidence & Commercial Playbook

## Hypothesis driven, not valuation driven
Each of ten ideas starts as an **unvalidated hypothesis**. Prices and market size claims are *not verified*. Build the smallest workflow which proves customer pain.

## Interview protocol
- Minimum target for first wave: 10 independent buyer interviews per niche before committing meaningful engineering. This is a management target, not a completed result.
- Ask for a recent instance of the job, time and money spent, old alternatives, frequency, authority to buy, constraints and data concerns.
- Ask for permission before collecting samples; sanitize any customer artifacts.
- Log `source/date/persona/problem/alternative/frequency/budget_signal/consent`. Public repo stores aggregated, redacted findings only.

## Paid pilot test
A real pilot requires objective scope, measurable baseline, signed terms if applicable, legitimate payment collection, support contact and explicit duration. Pilot conversion and cash receipt are distinct metrics.

## SaaS scorecard (evidence-based)
- Demand: buyer interviews completed, representative workflows inspected, qualified leads.
- Delivery: installation success, task completion rate, defect escape rate, time-to-first-value.
- Revenue: *actual* settled receipts, MRR (defined), refunds, churn, gross margin, cash conversion.
- Risk: data protection incidents, dependency concentration, owner hours/week, customer concentration.
- Transferability: use of third-party licensed code, vendor change, migration time and contract assignment.

## Pricing
Initial hypotheses are in product specs. Validate actual willingness to pay; do not conflate public competitor prices with your conversion rate.

## Sale scenarios
1. **Code/asset license**: license defined IP and usage with limits, no assertion of business revenue.
2. **Product asset sale**: code, brand (when owned), domain, documentation and transferable data subject to permissions.
3. **Operating SaaS business acquisition**: buyer inspects verifiable profits, retention, liabilities, churn and operator workload.

**Do not quote guaranteed exit prices.** Valuation methods depend on market evidence, costs, customer quality and deal terms. Deal documents require independent legal/tax review and Founder signature.
